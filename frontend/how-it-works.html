How Deep Image Prior Restores Thermal Images

An unsupervised paradigm where a convolutional neural network's architecture itself acts as an implicit regularizer for image inverse problems without requiring training datasets.

1. The DIP Inductive Bias

Standard deep learning for thermal image enhancement (e.g., DnCNN, ESRGAN) relies on supervised training across thousands of paired thermal images. In contrast, Deep Image Prior (Ulyanov et al.) proves that deep convolutional architectures have a profound intrinsic bias toward generating natural structures (smooth surfaces, continuous thermal gradients, sharp object boundaries) much faster than random noise or non-natural artifacts.

Given a degraded thermal image \(x_0\) and a valid pixel mask \(M\), we initialize a fixed noise tensor \(z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)\) and optimize the network parameters \(\theta\) exclusively on this single image:

min_θ || ( f_θ(z) - x_0 ) ⊙ M ||²_2 / sum(M)

2. Thermal Sensor Degradations

Uncooled microbolometer Focal Plane Arrays (FPAs) used in long-wave infrared (LWIR) cameras suffer from distinct physical failure modes that make classical spatial filtering inadequate:

  • Dead / Blind Pixels: Individual bolometer elements that are open-circuit or short-circuit, reading fixed 0.0 or saturated 1.0.
  • Readout Column Streaks: Gain and offset drift between readout integrated circuit (ROIC) columns causing vertical or horizontal damage streaks.
  • Fixed-Pattern Noise (FPN): Non-uniformity across microbolometer elements due to manufacturing variance.

3. 5-Level U-Net Generator Architecture

Our generator features an asymmetric 5-level encoder-decoder design. A crucial architectural feature from the original DIP paper is that skip connections pass through a 1×1 convolution that reduces the channel count to 4 before concatenation. This bottleneck prevents high-frequency noise from bypassing the network while allowing multi-scale structural guidance.
